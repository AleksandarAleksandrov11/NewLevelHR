A hire is not a success on the day the contract is signed. It is a success at
the end of the probation period, when both sides know the decision is right.
Between those two points sits the part most companies leave to chance.

## Day 1: agree what good looks like

Before the laptop arrives, write down what the role has to achieve in the first
month, who the person will work with and how often you will speak. Two sides of
a page is enough. It takes an hour and it removes the single biggest source of
early friction: two people with two different pictures of the same job.

## Day 30: name the friction while it is small

Thirty days in, everything is still fixable. Ask the direct questions: what is
unclear, what is slower than expected, where is the new person waiting on
someone else. Write down the answers and what changes because of them.

## Day 60: correct the course, not the person

By day 60 you know whether the scope was right. Sometimes the job on paper is
not the job that exists. Adjusting the scope at this point is cheap; discovering
it after probation is not.

## Day 90: make the decision on evidence

A probation review that ends in "let's see how it goes" helps nobody. Use the
scorecard you wrote on day 1: what was achieved, what was not, and what the
development plan looks like for the next quarter. If the answer is no, both
sides knew it was coming.

---

None of this is complicated. It is just structure that nobody owns when a small
company is busy growing. That is usually where we come in.
