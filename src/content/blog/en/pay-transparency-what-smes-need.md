The EU Pay Transparency Directive gets discussed as a reporting obligation.
For a company of twenty or eighty people, that is the smaller half of the story.

## What changes for candidates and employees

In general terms: candidates get the right to know the pay range before the
interview, and they may no longer be asked about their pay history. Employees
get the right to information about pay levels for comparable work. Reporting on
the gender pay gap is phased in depending on company size.

## What that means in practice

Guesswork pay stops working. If you cannot explain why two people doing
comparable work are paid differently, the directive is not your first problem,
it is only the moment the problem becomes visible.

The practical answer is small and boring:

1. Group the roles you have into a handful of levels.
2. Give each level a range, with a reason for the top and the bottom.
3. Write down what moves someone through the range.
4. Apply it to the next offer you make, not retroactively to everyone at once.
